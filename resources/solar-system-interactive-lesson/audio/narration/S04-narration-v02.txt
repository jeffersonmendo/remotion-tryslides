Now let us build the overview with transparent images and editable labels. Bring in the Sun and the eight planet images, then adjust their positions and sizes on the dark canvas. Keep each image in proportion, especially Saturn with its wider rings. We are arranging a teaching illustration, not drawing a scale model. Follow the sequence outward from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. In this composition, the arrangement curves around the available space instead of using a numbered grid. Earth sits between Venus and Mars. Next, add the planet names as separate text elements. Position each name close enough to identify its image, without hiding the surface or overlapping another label. The names matter because color alone is not a reliable instruction. A learner should not have to guess which blue object we mean. The image placement and labeling were recorded separately; together they build this overview. Repeated placement and typing can be shortened, while the useful actions remain clear. Before continuing, look at the whole diagram. Can you identify Earth from its label and its neighbors? Keep in mind that neither the displayed sizes nor the gaps represent real astronomical scale.
